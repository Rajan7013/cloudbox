Privacy Policy

What CloudBox accesses, where your files are stored and how to manage access.

CloudBox is an independent Android file manager for Google Drive. This policy explains how the app handles account information, files and local copies.

If you have any questions or feedback about this Privacy Policy, please contact our team directly at rajanprasaila@gmail.com.

1. Information CloudBox Accesses

Google Account Profile

When you sign in, CloudBox requests the following OAuth 2.0 scopes to identify you and display your profile within the app:

  • openid — Verifies your identity through Google's OpenID Connect.
  • email — Retrieves your email address so the app can display which account is connected.
  • profile — Retrieves your name and profile picture for display in the app.

Google Drive Access

CloudBox uses the drive.file scope — a least-privilege scope that grants access only to files and folders that:

  • You create or upload through CloudBox, or
  • You explicitly open or select within CloudBox.

This means CloudBox cannot see or modify files in your Drive that you have not interacted with through the app.

2. How Data Is Used & Stored

CloudBox uses Firebase Authentication for sign-in and connects directly to Google Drive to access authorized files. The developer does not operate an intermediary file-storage server.

On your device, CloudBox stores the following data:

  • Offline files — Cached copies of files you mark for offline access.
  • Viewer cache — Temporary data used by built-in viewers (images, PDFs, text).
  • Camera captures — Photos taken within the app for document scanning, stored locally until uploaded to your Drive.
  • Room database — Local metadata cache for fast navigation and search.
  • DataStore preferences — Your app settings (theme, sort order, etc.).
  • Reader data — Private annotations, bookmarks and reading preferences are stored locally. Images you export remain in the selected folder or gallery until you delete them.
Google API Services User Data Policy Compliance

CloudBox's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

3. Android Permissions Explained

CloudBox requests only the minimum Android permissions needed to function:

  • INTERNET — Required to communicate with Google Drive APIs and authenticate your Google account.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — Used to detect network availability (e.g., for the Wi-Fi-only upload safeguard).
  • CAMERA (optional, requested at runtime) — Only requested when you use the "Take Photo" or "Scan Document" features. You can deny this permission and still use all other features normally.
  • App updates — CloudBox checks its published version manifest on GitHub or the CloudBox website. Android asks for approval before installing an APK update.

No Broad Storage Permissions

CloudBox does not request READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, or MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE. File selection uses Android's built-in Photo Picker and document picker, which require zero additional permissions.

4. Advertising and diagnostics

CloudBox does not display ads or sell user data. Scanned-page text recognition runs on your device. Google ML Kit can send diagnostic and usage metrics to Google. See Google ML Kit terms.

5. Data Retention & How to Delete / Revoke Access

You can manage device copies and Google account access separately:

  • Clear cache — Go to your device Settings → Apps → CloudBox → Clear Cache to remove temporary viewer data.
  • Sign out — Sign out within the app to disconnect your Google account.
  • Uninstall — Uninstalling CloudBox removes all locally stored data (offline files, database, preferences).
  • Revoke access — Visit myaccount.google.com/permissions to revoke CloudBox's access to your Google account at any time.

Files stored in your Google Drive remain in your Drive and are governed by Google's Terms of Service.
